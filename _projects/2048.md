My Cambridge dissertation, and the piece of work I am most fond of. It produced the
world's highest-scoring algorithm for the 2048 puzzle game, along with the fastest
simulation engine for it — the two being related, since you cannot train what you
cannot simulate quickly enough.

The agent reaches the 2048-tile within 1.2 milliseconds in 99.98% of games, and
reaches the 32768-tile — a tile most players never see — in 75% of games.

How it works, briefly:

- a custom **200M-parameter RAMnet**, trained by self-play reinforcement learning
  over 150 million games;
- **expectimax search** over the stochastic tile placements;
- critical phases of the board, where a single mistake ends the run, are handled by
  **local brute force** instead, maximising the probability of survival rather than
  the expected score.

It was marked second-best dissertation in a cohort of 120, at 92.5%.
