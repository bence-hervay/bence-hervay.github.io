I build quantitative models and the infrastructure they run on. Right now that means
detecting and refining financially interpretable factors for predicting covariance
matrices at AGITProp in London, where I own most of the mathematical components of
the pipeline.

Before that I read Computer Science at Trinity College, Cambridge. My dissertation
was on [reinforcement learning for 2048](/projects/2048/) — the highest-scoring
algorithm for the game so far, and the fastest simulation engine to train it with.
Since 2024 I have been working on [Rupert's problem](/projects/ruperts-problem/), a
350-year-old question about whether every convex polyhedron fits through a hole cut
in itself.

Most of what I make is written from first principles, without leaning on high-level
libraries — a chess engine, a path tracer, an automatic differentiation framework, a
muon tomograph. Away from a screen I speedsolve Rubik's cubes, play ultimate frisbee,
and I am always happy to be handed a good puzzle.

The [CV](/cv/) has the full version and a [PDF](/cv.pdf) to take away.
The write-ups live in [projects](/projects/), anything you can actually play with
will appear in [apps](/apps/), and [contact](/contact/) has the ways to reach me.
